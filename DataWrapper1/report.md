## Visualization 1: Line Chart

![Visualization 1](Visualizations/Viz1.png)

**Rationale:** Line Chart makes the changes in tuition amounts easy to understand visually between 2006/2007 to 2025/2026. Having the values for Average Canadian Undergraduate Tuition for Canada and Alberta on the same graph allows us to evaluate and assess the trends and see when the Average Tuition for Alberta was higher or lower than the Canadian average. The values have been adjusted for inflation, which makes the comparison across these years more significant. Insights : According to the line chart, the tuition price (CAD) increased in both Alberta and Canada, however the underlying trends varied. Alberta’s average tuition was lowest in 2019/2020 before rising again, while the Canadian average tuition was at its peak in 2018/2019 and then declined to $6,435 in 2022/2023, then recovered to $6,706 in 2025/2026. By 2025/2026, Alberta’s average tuition was $6,995 in comparison to Canadian average tuition of $6,706. Hence, the total difference between the two values was $289. The Pro here is that the line chart makes it easier to compare Alberta and Canada over time and the con is that as the y-axis does not start at 0 so the differences in values between Alberta and Canada can appear visually larger than they actually are.

## Visualization 2: Scatter Plot

![Visualization 2](Visualizations/Viz2.png)

**Rationale:** Scatter plot here compares the international undergraduate tuition within Alberta with the overall Canadian average. Here, each dot displays a single academic year, making it easier to visualize the relationship and trends between the tuition levels. We can also view the dots that are different from the overall pattern. Since the graph uses inflation adjusted Canadian Dollars, it makes these comparisons more insightful. Insights: The dots on the scatter plot have a positive relationship as the higher Canadian Tuition averages co-exist with higher Alberta Averages. However, from 2015/2016 to 2019/2020, Alberta’s tuition stayed rather stagnant around $22,800 whereas the Canadian values increased from $24,994 to $31,113. In 2025/2026, the Canadian average tuition was higher ($36,199) as compared to Alberta’s average tuition ($30,245) with a difference of $5,954 between the two values. The pro here is that we can quickly identify whether higher Canadian tuition is associated with increase of tuition amounts in Alberta and the con is that it can be difficult to make precise comparisons as the emphasis is on the overall pattern.

## Visualization 3: Dot Plot

![Visualization 3](Visualizations/Viz3.png)

**Rationale:** I utilised a dot plot as it compares Alberta’s Tuition with Canadian Tuition across the following four student groups in 2025-2026. 1) Canadian Undergraduate 2) Canadian Graduate 3) International Undergraduate 4) International Graduate Placing these dots on the same row makes it visually easier to demarcate the differences within each group. Here the shared scale is useful as comparisons can be made across the four groups. The two different colors - light and dark blue also clearly differentiate Alberta from Canada. Insights: Both Alberta ($30,245) and Canada ($36,199) have the highest International undergraduate tuition across the four groups. Alberta’s tuition was lower than the Canadian Average in 3 out of 4 groups. However, the Canadian Undergraduate tuition for Alberta was higher than the Canadian Average by $289.The highest gap between the Canadian and Albertan tuition values was for International Graduates with Alberta’s Tuition about $6,103 below the Canadian National Tuition Average value. Here, the pro of dot plot is that it allows us to distinguish between various categories on the same scale at the same time and the con is that small differences can be hard to differentiate when two values are close together.

## Visualization 4: Column Chart

![Visualization 4](Visualizations/Viz4.png)

**Rationale:** The Column Chart here displays the trends in Alberta’s International Undergraduate tuition amounts for each academic year. Here, the columns are separate from each other which makes it easier to compare the yearly amounts. It is also helpful in spotting periods of sudden increase/decrease or periods where the tuition amounts remained fairly stable. As I begin the vertical axis at 0, it keeps the differences in column heights proportional to the tuition amounts. Insights: Inflation adjusted tuition increased from $16,376 in 2006/2007 to $30,245 in 2025/2026, approximately 84.7% increase. The international undergraduate tuition in Alberta remained close to $22,800 between 2015/2016 till 2019/2020 until rising again in 2020/2021 to $28,101. After two minor declines, the tuition value increased again and peaked in 2025-2026. The pro of the column chart is that the zero baseline makes the column heights proportional to the tuition amounts, leading to clear comparisons. However, one of the cons is that comparing non-adjacent years requires scanning across several columns, making change over longer periods harder to visualize than differences between neighbouring years.

## Visualization 5: Horizontal Bar Chart

![Visualization 5](Visualizations/Viz5.png)

**Rationale:** I used a horizontal bar chart to compare the percentage change in inflation-adjusted tuition between 2006/2007 and 2025/2026 across eight combinations of regions and student groups. Focusing on percentage change allows us to notice comparisons relative to the starting tuition for each group. The order of the bars is from the largest increase towards the largest decrease which makes the rankings clear. Horizontal labels and the percentage change labels make the chart easy to understand and interpret. Insights: There was a percentage increase of 108.5% in Canada’s international undergraduate tuition which is followed by Alberta at 84.7%. The international graduate tuition fee increased by 44.7% but by only 17.4% in the province of Alberta. The Canadian Undergraduate tuition fee in Canada and Alberta had a modest increase of 17.4% and 13.2% respectively. We can clearly see that Canadian Graduate tuition fee across Alberta and Canada decreased in both regions decreasing by 17.2% and 1.1% respectively.The pro of the horizontal bar chart is that because of the shared percentage scale it is easier to compare the proportional increase and decrease across groups. The con of using this chart is that utilising only the first and the last years hides fluctuations in the middle years so we are unable to recognize if the increase or decrease was sharp, sudden or slow and steady.
